The Quantified Self is the practice and informal movement of tracking personal data — sleep, heart rate, mood, productivity, biomarkers, glucose, location — as a tool for self-understanding and behaviour change. The phrase was coined in 2007 by *Wired* editors Gary Wolf and Kevin Kelly to describe a regularly meeting San Francisco group of self-trackers, and it became the umbrella term for the broader movement that followed.

Practitioners range across a wide spectrum. Some are casual users of mainstream wearables (Fitbit, Apple Watch, Garmin) tracking steps and sleep; others run rigorous personal experiments with continuous glucose monitors, EEG headsets, custom data pipelines, and quarterly lab-test panels. The movement's recurring slogan is "self knowledge through numbers". Local Quantified Self meetups follow a distinctive format — three-question presentations ("What did you do? How did you do it? What did you learn?") — that resist commercial pitch and centre lived experience.

The Quantified Self sits inside the broader [[biohacking]] movement and shares the conviction that the person being measured should be the primary recipient of the measurement, not merely its subject. That conviction depends on [[self-custody|self-custody of data]], [[open-source]] tooling, and the right to read the formats one's own body emits. The sharpest tension is with the same wearable industry that supplies most of the hardware: many devices ingest data the user cannot export, and a movement nominally about personal sovereignty often runs on infrastructure that maximises corporate access to the same data.
